# Contact rules are reported beside the Score, never inside it

Each Business carries a Contact Rule: whether a Rep may email it without asking first, and what a call or email must include, under the law of its country. The rule is shown on the row and in the drawer. It does not change the Score.

Folding it in was the simpler design: a Business a Rep may not email is harder to reach, and a lower Score would push it down the list. We rejected it because it confuses two questions. Under PECR a sole trader counts as an individual and needs consent before marketing email, while a limited company does not; but a sole trader is no less likely to need a website. Ranking it lower would bury a good prospect for a reason that has nothing to do with fit, and would hide that it can still be reached another way. Kept apart, the Score says how good a prospect is and the Contact Rule says how to approach it.

The rules are drawn from each regulator's published guidance, cited on every verdict, and presented as guidance rather than legal advice. Where they depend on a fact we lack, they take the more protected case: the ICO's own instruction is that a business of unknown type should be treated as an individual. That is also why a UK Business we cannot match to Companies House is shown as needing consent.

**Consequences**: The rules cover email in the UK, US and Pakistan, and telephone calls in the UK, where every number must be screened against TPS and CTPS first. Calls in the US and Pakistan were not researched, so the tool says nothing about them rather than guessing. A UK Business's email rule is only as good as its Companies House match, so without a key, UK Businesses fall back to needing consent.
